What this is. A feed on the medical industries worldwide, in twenty subjects, scoped to what the section it follows documents. Who pays the prescriber, and the link it quantifies between payment and prescription. What gets prescribed as a result. Diagnosing more, and the widening of the criteria that makes that possible. Unnecessary procedures with a financial motive. What treatment itself does — adverse events, withdrawals, recalls. Trial data and what never gets published. How approval works and who sits on the committees. The door between regulator and industry. Marketing and off-label promotion. Who writes the clinical guidelines. Pricing, patents and access. Billing, cover and refusal. Devices and implants. Dependence. Contaminated and unsafe products. What is done where oversight is thinnest. Health records as an asset. Mental health treatment. What litigation actually costs them. And prevention, and what is set against all of it. Nothing here is written or summarised: headlines and snippets are the publishers' own, every row links to the original, and no model touches the text.

Payment and prescription are separate subjects on purpose. The section's argument runs through a chain — money reaches the prescriber, prescribing changes, diagnosis widens to justify it — and the figures it cites sit at different links. Who pays the prescriber follows the transfers themselves, the disclosure regimes meant to record them, and the databases that publish them. What gets prescribed follows the outcome: brand-name share, prescribing rates, inappropriate and unnecessary prescribing. Keeping them apart means a story about a disclosure rule and a story about prescribing volume do not collapse into one chip.

What it is not, and an important limit. This is a feed on the conduct of an industry. It is not medical advice and is not built to be read as any. The health-and-wellness pages are refused outright — symptom explainers, supplements, remedies, what-causes pieces, diet and sleep advice — as are individual and celebrity illness stories, and business coverage with no conduct angle. Nothing here should be used to make a decision about your own treatment; that is a conversation for a clinician who knows your case. A story no subject will claim is refused rather than filed under a name it did not earn.

Standing. Every wire is labelled by what kind of source it is, and every row carries the label. Medicines regulators, courts & inquiries is the bodies that approve, police and investigate — the WHO, medicines agencies, prosecutors, courts and public inquiries. Research, journals & evidence review is the trial and evidence work, the payments and pricing research. Patient & access organisations is those arguing from the receiving end, on access, harm and health data. Press is general news across 26 languages. Standing is provenance, not a claim that a story is important.

Weight. Separately, each story is scored on what it carries: a decision — a ruling, a settlement, a fine, an approval granted or revoked, a recall, a licence struck off (2); institutional material — a WHO or regulator finding, a public inquiry report, a systematic review or randomised trial, official statistics (2); a measured figure such as a prescribing rate, an odds ratio or a settlement value (1); a pending decision with a date attached (1); a named jurisdiction (1); and a primary source (1). At three or more the row is marked consequential.

What is refused. Health-and-wellness coverage, symptom explainers and remedies, individual and celebrity illness stories, business coverage with no conduct angle, sponsored content and market copy. The status line reports how many stories each harvest refused.

Translation. The Translate control at the top renders the headlines and snippets on screen into any of 26 languages, through a free public translation endpoint — no account, no key, nothing stored. A translated row is marked as machine translation and keeps the publisher's original wording on hover, because a machine rendering is not the publisher's words. If the endpoint is unavailable the row simply stays in its original language rather than emptying. Translation applies to what is on screen; scroll further or widen a filter and press the control again.

Region. Placement runs three levels deep. Pick a region and a second row opens with its subregions; pick one of those and a third row lists the places inside it — Latin America to the Amazon Basin to the Brazilian Amazon, Oceania to Australia to the Northern Territory. Each row counts what it holds, so an empty subregion is visible rather than hidden, and every story files under the subregion and region above whatever place it names.

Limits worth knowing. The gate is mechanical: it reads words, not meaning. Standing is assigned per wire rather than per article. Google News caps a query at roughly 100 results over about 30 days. Coverage is uneven by language and the counts show it rather than hiding it.

Every wire read on the last harvest, with its standing: what it returned, and how many of its stories were refused. A wire that could not be reached says so rather than disappearing.
